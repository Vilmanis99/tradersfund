<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Verdict</strong></h2>

<p>Rule-tolerant discretionary traders should buy OFP's $35–$535 One Phase evaluation and ignore the Instant Plus tiers.</p>

<div class="key-takeaways">
  <div class="title">Fast take</div>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Nine products, 45 priced tiers, $35 to $1,500</strong> — captured from ofpfunding.com on 2026-07-27.</li>
    <li><strong>80% profit split is the base price.</strong> A 100% split is sold on every tier for roughly 20% more — the $100K One Phase is $299 at 80% and $358.80 at 100%.</li>
    <li><strong>Static max drawdown.</strong> "This limit never changes during the challenge"; the help centre rebases it after each approved reward.</li>
    <li>Daily loss is measured off the <strong>previous day's closing equity</strong>, recorded at midnight GMT+2.</li>
    <li><strong>Total manageable accounts are capped at $300,000</strong>, and account merging is not supported.</li>
    <li>Two Phase at $200K is the cheapest risk-adjusted entry OFP sells: $385 for an R-multiple of 0.02.</li>
    <li><strong>Trustpilot has suppressed OFP's aggregate rating</strong> — "This company's rating is unavailable due to a breach of our guidelines" (verified 2026-07-27).</li>
    <li>Instant Plus is sold on the pricing widget but has <strong>no rules section at all</strong> on OFP's own /trading-rules page.</li>
  </ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How the rules actually work</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Max drawdown is static, and OFP says so plainly:</strong> "Maximum Overall Loss = Initial Balance x Max Overall Loss Percentage" and "This limit never changes during the challenge." On a $100,000 One Phase at 6%, the hard floor is $94,000 from the first trade to the last, whether your equity peaks at $101,000 or $118,000. The one wrinkle is on the funded side, where the help centre states the rule "is calculated against your highest balance at the start of the period or after your last Reward" — so the floor is static within a payout cycle and rebases upward after each approved withdrawal.</p>

<p><strong>Daily loss is end-of-day equity based, not intraday balance based.</strong> The published formula is "Maximum Daily Loss = Equity (recorded at end of previous day) x Max Daily Loss Percentage", with equity stamped at midnight GMT+2. On that same $100,000 One Phase at 3% daily, day one gives you a $3,000 allowance. Close day one at $102,000 and day two's allowance becomes $3,060 measured down from $102,000, so your intraday floor that day is $98,940 — well above the $94,000 static floor. Profits raise the daily line; they never move the overall one.</p>

<p><strong>The consistency rule is the one number OFP does not state consistently.</strong> The formula is published: "Inconsistency Score = Best Day Closed PnL / Total Closed PnL x 100". The threshold is not. OFP's pricing payload carries a per-product ceiling of 15% on One Phase and Two Phase, 20% on Instant Classic, and none at all on Lite, Plus, Pro and Prime. The help centre instead ties the threshold to the payout cycle: "On-Demand Accounts: Your score must be under 10%. Bi-Weekly Accounts: Your score must be under 15%. Monthly Accounts: Your score must be under 20%." Those two sources contradict each other — Instant Classic pays bi-weekly, which the help centre caps at 15%, while the pricing data says 20%. We have left the field null rather than pick a winner. What both sources agree on is the consequence: it is a soft rule, "you won't lose the account if the score exceeds the recommended", but "you are not entitled to request a payout".</p>

<p><strong>News trading is permitted with a four-minute blackout.</strong> OFP's wording: "Trading during economic news releases is permitted", but "Opening new trades is prohibited from 2 minutes before until 2 minutes after the release of high-impact economic news." Existing positions are not the target — opening new ones inside that window is.</p>

<p><strong>Copy trading is a closure offence.</strong> The rule covers "mirroring trades between accounts, whether internal (between OFP accounts) or external", and explicitly "includes copying trades across multiple accounts owned by the same trader", with a stated consequence of "immediate account closure". Expert Advisors are restricted rather than banned: what is prohibited is "publicly distributed or commercially available third-party Expert Advisors designed to exploit platform inefficiencies or coordinated trading behavior". A private algorithm you wrote is not what that sentence describes; a downloaded latency EA is.</p>

<p><strong>Two rules are simply not published.</strong> Neither ofpfunding.com/trading-rules nor the OFP help centre states a weekend-holding rule or an overnight/swap rule anywhere. If your strategy carries positions through Friday's close, get that in writing from support before funding an account — an unpublished rule is not the same as a permissive one. Separately, 28 consecutive days without a trade suspends the account.</p>

<p><strong>Instant Plus has no rules page.</strong> OFP's /trading-rules page publishes sections for Instant Lite, Instant Classic, Instant Pro, Instant Prime, One Phase and Two Phase. Instant Plus is sold on the pricing widget at up to $1,500 per account and appears in none of them. Its drawdown figures come from the pricing data; its product-specific rules are unverified.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Payout speed in practice</strong></h2>

<p>The first reward request may be submitted <strong>14 calendar days after the first trade is executed</strong> — on the One Phase product, specifically 14 days after the first trade on the Master Account, not the evaluation. After that, "traders may request bi-weekly rewards" on eight of the nine products. Instant Lite is the exception: a 30-day account duration, monthly payout day, and a single reward request in total.</p>

<p>Withdrawals are "typically processed within an average timeframe of 48 hours". Crypto and bank transfers are free below $500; above $500 the payment runs through Rise and carries a <strong>fixed $50 fee</strong>. That fee structure inverts the usual incentive — a trader withdrawing $600 loses 8.3% of it to the fee, while a trader withdrawing $5,000 loses 1%. Batching payouts is materially cheaper than taking them as they clear.</p>

<p><strong>Trustpilot has suppressed OFP Funding's aggregate rating.</strong> As of 2026-07-27, <a href="https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ofpfunding.com">the firm's Trustpilot profile</a> displays "This company's rating is unavailable due to a breach of our guidelines" in place of a score. That is a platform enforcement action against review-collection conduct, not a finding about payouts, and Trustpilot does not publish which guideline was breached. We report it because it is the single most material change to OFP's public trust signal, and because a suppressed rating removes the one third-party metric most traders use to sanity-check a firm. Treat any OFP star rating quoted elsewhere as stale until the profile is restored.</p>

<p>OFP publishes a scaling path rather than a fixed ladder: "20% total profit over three months" earns "25% return on your initial deposit". Growth is bounded by the $300,000 total manageable-account cap, and because account merging is not supported, that cap is not reachable by stacking two $200K accounts into one book.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>FAQ</strong></h2>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What does OFP Funding actually cost?</strong></h3>
<p>Between $35 and $1,500 across 45 priced tiers, at the 80% profit-split base price. The cheapest entries are the $5K One Phase and $5K Instant Classic at $35 each; the most expensive is the $100K Instant Plus (5%/10%) at $1,500. A 100% split adds roughly 20% to any of them.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why does OFP have no Trustpilot rating?</strong></h3>
<p>Trustpilot has suppressed it. The profile shows "This company's rating is unavailable due to a breach of our guidelines" as of 2026-07-27. Trustpilot applies this to review-collection conduct and does not publish the specific guideline breached; it is not a statement about whether OFP pays out.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Is OFP's drawdown trailing or static?</strong></h3>
<p>Static. The firm's own wording is "a 'hard' limit based on your initial balance" that "never changes during the challenge". On the funded side the help centre rebases it to your balance after each approved reward, so it steps up between payout cycles but never trails your equity intraday.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What is OFP's consistency rule?</strong></h3>
<p>The formula is "Best Day Closed PnL / Total Closed PnL x 100"; the threshold depends on which OFP source you read. The pricing data sets 15% on One Phase and Two Phase and 20% on Instant Classic, while the help centre sets 10%, 15% and 20% by payout cycle. Either way it is a soft rule — it blocks a payout request rather than closing the account.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Can I scale past $300,000 at OFP?</strong></h3>
<p>No. OFP states "the total manageable accounts are capped at $300,000" and that "account merging is not supported". The scaling path — "20% total profit over three months" for "25% return on your initial deposit" — operates below that ceiling.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Is the OFP fee refundable?</strong></h3>
<p>Only within 14 calendar days and only if you have not traded. The terms grant a refund "provided that the account has not been used and no trading activity has occurred" and withdraw it "once any trade has been placed".</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Can US residents use OFP Funding?</strong></h3>
<p>OFP states its MetaTrader 5 services are "not intended for U.S. citizens/residents". All OFP accounts are simulated, and the entity is OFP Funding Ltd, registered in St. Lucia as company 2024-00577.</p>
