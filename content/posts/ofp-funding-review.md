<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How the rules actually work</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Max drawdown is static, and OFP says so plainly:</strong> "Maximum Overall Loss = Initial Balance x Max Overall Loss Percentage" and "This limit never changes during the challenge." On a $100,000 Instant Classic (3%/6%), the hard floor is $94,000 from the first trade to the last, whether your equity peaks at $101,000 or $118,000. The one wrinkle is on the funded side, where the help centre states the rule "is calculated against your highest balance at the start of the period or after your last Reward" — so the floor is static within a payout cycle and rebases upward after each approved withdrawal.</p>

<p><strong>Daily loss is end-of-day equity based, not intraday balance based.</strong> The published formula is "Maximum Daily Loss = Equity (recorded at end of previous day) x Max Daily Loss Percentage", with equity stamped at midnight GMT+2. On that same $100,000 Instant Classic at 3% daily, day one gives you a $3,000 allowance. Close day one at $102,000 and day two's allowance becomes $3,060 measured down from $102,000, so your intraday floor that day is $98,940 — well above the $94,000 static floor. Profits raise the daily line; they never move the overall one.</p>

<p><strong>The consistency rule is the one number OFP does not state consistently.</strong> The formula is published: the help centre (re-read 2026-10-29) writes "Consistency Score = (Best Trading Day Profit ÷ Total Profit) × 100", and the trading-rules page uses the closed-PnL form of the same idea. OFP's pricing data still carries a consistency value of 20% for Instant Classic and none for Instant Lite or Instant Plus. The help-centre article no longer lists the older 10% / 15% / 20% caps by payout cycle; it does say that a flagged account means "you are not entitled to request a payout", even when other rules were respected. We leave the structured threshold unresolved rather than invent a number the live help page no longer prints.</p>

<p><strong>News trading is permitted with a four-minute blackout.</strong> OFP's wording: "Trading during economic news releases is permitted", but "Opening new trades is prohibited from 2 minutes before until 2 minutes after the release of high-impact economic news." Existing positions are not the target — opening new ones inside that window is.</p>

<p><strong>Copy trading is a closure offence.</strong> The rule covers "mirroring trades between accounts, whether internal (between OFP accounts) or external", and explicitly "includes copying trades across multiple accounts owned by the same trader", with a stated consequence of "immediate account closure". Expert Advisors are restricted rather than banned: what is prohibited is "publicly distributed or commercially available third-party Expert Advisors designed to exploit platform inefficiencies or coordinated trading behavior". A private algorithm you wrote is not what that sentence describes; a downloaded latency EA is.</p>

<p><strong>Two rules are simply not published.</strong> Neither ofpfunding.com/trading-rules nor the OFP help centre states a weekend-holding rule or an overnight/swap rule anywhere. If your strategy carries positions through Friday's close, get that in writing from support before funding an account — an unpublished rule is not the same as a permissive one. Separately, 28 consecutive days without a trade suspends the account.</p>

<p><strong>Instant Plus rules are only partly documented.</strong> OFP's /trading-rules page now has tabs for Instant Lite, Instant Classic and Instant Plus, but the Instant Plus tab's product-specific text was not captured in the 2026-10-14 recapture. Its prices, drawdown, minimum days and payout cycle come from OFP's pricing data; treat any other Instant Plus rule as unverified until you read that tab yourself.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>True cost to break even</strong></h2>

<p>Break-even profit is the fee divided by your profit split: the trading gain you must produce before your 80% share of it repays what you spent. The R-multiple sets that figure against the dollar value of your maximum drawdown. R below 1 means the firm lets you lose more getting there than you need to make. <h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How the OFP Funding payout process works</strong></h3>

<p>OFP calls payouts "Rewards", and its help centre (pages rechecked on 2026-10-14) describes the same five steps for every plan:</p>

<ol class="wp-block-list">
  <li><strong>Trading and review period.</strong> You trade the account, and OFP reviews it at the end of the period.</li>
  <li><strong>Eligibility check.</strong> The account must respect the daily drawdown, maximum drawdown, Inconsistency Score and prohibited-strategy rules; "only profits that meet all conditions are eligible".</li>
  <li><strong>Reward calculation.</strong> If your Delta (net profit after rules) is positive, OFP applies your plan's profit split.</li>
  <li><strong>Payout form.</strong> You submit the form from the dashboard or by answering the payout email. OFP self-bills: its terms authorise it to issue the invoice on your behalf, so the name and payment details must match your account.</li>
  <li><strong>Payment.</strong> OFP says the invoice is issued within 3 calendar days of receiving the form and payment is sent within 2 calendar days of issuance, "usually 2-3 business days from the date of your request". Processing runs Monday to Friday, 9 AM to 5 PM CET, and busy periods can take up to 3 business days. Payouts are made in USD.</li>
</ol>

<p><strong>Payment methods and fees.</strong> Payouts under $500 go by bank transfer or USDC crypto (ERC-20 or Polygon only), and the trading-rules page says both "are processed without fees" below $500. Payouts over $500 go through Rise with a fixed $50 fee "deducted directly from the payout amount", regardless of size. That fixed fee inverts the usual incentive: a $600 withdrawal loses 8.3% to it, a $5,000 withdrawal loses 1%, so batching payouts is cheaper than taking each one as it clears.</p>

<p><strong>Two OFP timelines.</strong> The trading-rules page says withdrawals are "typically processed within an average timeframe of 48 hours", while the help centre says "usually 2-3 business days from the date of your request". Plan for the longer figure.</p>

<p><strong>What delays or denies a payout.</strong> OFP says payouts "can be subject to additional analysis at any moment, which may require you to explain your trading strategy", and are paid only "when a real trading strategy is present". Its list of common delays includes an incomplete form, details that don't match your registered name, a rule breach under review, incomplete KYC, and third-party payment accounts. You can keep trading once you have answered the payout email.</p>
