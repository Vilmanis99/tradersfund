<p><strong>Our conclusion as of 28 July 2026:</strong> an Indian trader should not select an ITR form merely because a prop firm calls a payment a “reward,” “performance fee,” or “payout.” The return follows the legal and tax facts. A chartered accountant should review the contract, taxpayer residence status, payor entity, source analysis, and any business or professional activity before selecting the income head and schedules.</p>

<p>This guide is an operational record-keeping framework, not tax advice. It does not conclude that every prop-firm receipt is foreign-source income, that every payment provider creates a foreign asset, or that one return form applies to every trader.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Step 1: Identify the Contract and the Payor</strong></h2>

<p>Start with the legal document rather than the provider notification. Record the firm, exact payor legal entity, payor country, contract version, account product, and payout statement. If the money arrives from a different entity, ask the firm to explain that relationship in writing before filing.</p>

<p>The same brand can use different entities, contracts, or reward models across products. Save the contract that governed the specific payout instead of replacing it with the firm’s current website terms. Our <a href="/best-prop-firms-in-india/challenge-comparison">India challenge-rules comparison</a> separates the products, while the <a href="/best-prop-firms-in-india/payout-methods">India payout-methods comparison</a> shows published rails and known evidence gaps; neither surface assigns a tax classification.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Step 2: Choose the Return Family After Classifying the Income</strong></h2>

<p>The Income Tax Department’s current <a href="https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/file-itr-1-sahaj-online" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">ITR-1 guidance</a> says ITR-1 cannot be used by a person with income from any source outside India or profits and gains from business or profession. That rule eliminates the shortcut of choosing ITR-1 before analysing the receipt.</p>

<p>The Department’s <a href="https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/file-itr-2-online" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">ITR-2 guidance</a> expressly excludes business or professional income. Its <a href="https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual-business-profession?mobile-app=1" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">business and profession overview</a> describes ITR-3 as the return for an eligible individual or HUF with business or professional income. These boundaries do not decide the prop-firm classification; they show why classification must come first.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Step 3: Test Schedule FSI, Schedule TR, and Schedule FA Separately</strong></h2>

<p>The Income Tax Department’s <a href="https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/nudge/nudge-schedule-fa" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">foreign-income and foreign-assets hub</a> separates three schedules. Schedule FSI covers income from a source outside India for relevant resident taxpayers. Schedule TR is used for tax-relief details. Schedule FA covers specified foreign assets, accounts, and income and has separate residence-status rules.</p>

<p>Do not merge those three questions. A foreign payor does not automatically prove that a provider balance is a reportable foreign asset, and a Wise, Rise, crypto, or bank route does not automatically determine the source of income. The Department’s <a href="https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/sites/default/files/2026-02/Step%20by%20Step%20Guide%20FA%20FSI.pdf" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Schedule FA, FSI, and TR guide</a> says Schedule FA is not required for a person who is not ordinarily resident or is a non-resident; a CA should apply the current rules to the taxpayer’s residence status and actual accounts or assets.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Step 4: Use Form 67 Only for a Real Foreign Tax Credit Claim</strong></h2>

<p>The Department’s <a href="https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/newformpage/forms/from67-UM" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Form 67 user manual</a> describes the form for a resident taxpayer claiming credit for foreign tax paid outside India. Record foreign tax only when a document identifies tax actually paid or withheld and the taxpayer is eligible to claim credit.</p>

<p>A provider fee, correspondent-bank charge, wallet fee, gas fee, or currency-conversion spread is not foreign tax merely because it reduced the net payment. Keep each amount in a separate field. Ask the CA whether Form 67, Schedule FSI, and Schedule TR apply; do not create a foreign-tax amount from the difference between the gross and net receipt.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>A Five-Document Reconciliation Workflow</strong></h2>

<ol class="wp-block-list">
  <li><strong>Contract:</strong> save the signed or accepted version showing the reward relationship and responsible legal entity.</li>
  <li><strong>Payout approval:</strong> save the firm statement or dashboard record showing the gross reward and payout date.</li>
  <li><strong>Provider statement:</strong> record the provider transaction ID, route, fees, currency, and net amount.</li>
  <li><strong>FX evidence:</strong> preserve the receipt-date rate and named source used to prepare the INR working value.</li>
  <li><strong>Bank or wallet receipt:</strong> save the final credit advice, bank reference, or wallet transaction record and reconcile it to the provider statement.</li>
</ol>

<p>If the five documents do not reconcile, do not fill the gap with an assumption. Ask the firm or provider for a statement explaining the payor, deduction, exchange rate, or third-party transfer. Record the unresolved item in the notes column and show it to the CA.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Six Record-Keeping Mistakes to Avoid</strong></h2>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
  <li><strong>Using only a dashboard screenshot:</strong> it may omit the payor entity, fee, currency, and banking reference.</li>
  <li><strong>Recording only the net credit:</strong> it hides the gross reward and makes provider fees or foreign tax impossible to verify.</li>
  <li><strong>Treating a provider fee as foreign tax:</strong> Form 67 requires a foreign-tax-credit analysis, not a balancing figure.</li>
  <li><strong>Choosing the ITR from the marketing label:</strong> “reward” does not decide whether the facts belong under business or profession or another head.</li>
  <li><strong>Assuming crypto changes the reporting duty:</strong> the rail alone does not make a receipt tax-free or select an income head.</li>
  <li><strong>Deleting an old contract:</strong> a later terms page may not prove the agreement that governed an earlier payout.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Questions to Give Your Chartered Accountant</strong></h2>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
  <li>Which legal entity paid the reward, and how does that entity relate to the contract?</li>
  <li>What is the taxpayer’s residence status for the relevant year?</li>
  <li>Where does the income arise or accrue under the facts of this contract?</li>
  <li>Does the activity amount to profits and gains from business or profession or another income head?</li>
  <li>Which of Schedule FSI, Schedule TR, or Schedule FA applies, if any?</li>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>FAQ</strong></h2>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Is a prop-firm payout tax-free in India?</strong></h3>
<p>No blanket exemption follows from the phrase “prop-firm payout.” The contract, residence status, source, activity, and payor must be classified by a qualified adviser.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Can I report a prop-firm payout in ITR-1?</strong></h3>
<p>Do not assume so. The Income Tax Department says ITR-1 cannot be used when a person has income from a source outside India or profits and gains from business or profession.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Is ITR-2 or ITR-3 the right form?</strong></h3>
<p>ITR-2 excludes profits and gains from business or profession, while ITR-3 can apply when that income is present. A chartered accountant must classify the receipt before selecting the return.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Do I need Schedule FSI or Schedule FA?</strong></h3>
<p>Not automatically because the firm or provider is foreign. Residence status, source analysis, and the existence of a reportable foreign account, asset, or income item must each be tested.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Do I file Form 67 for a Wise, Rise, bank, or crypto fee?</strong></h3>
<p>No. Form 67 relates to a foreign tax credit claim. A payment-provider, bank, wallet, gas, or conversion fee is not foreign tax withheld.</p>
