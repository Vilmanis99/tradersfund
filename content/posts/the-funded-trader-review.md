<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Verdict</strong></h2>

<p>Buy the Classic 2-Step at $479 for $100K — it is one of only two TFT products with a published 80% split.</p>

<div class="key-takeaways">
  <div class="title">Fast take</div>
  <ul>
    <li>Seven live products as of 2026-07-27: Knight, Knight Pro, Classic 1-Step, Royal, Royal Pro, Classic 2-Step, Dragon. Prices run $42 to $1,100.</li>
    <li><strong>Only Classic 1-Step and Classic 2-Step publish a profit split (80%).</strong> The other five state "Up to 99%" — a ceiling, not a base rate.</li>
    <li>The help centre at help.thefundedtraderprogram.com returns HTTP 404 on every URL, including the per-challenge guides the firm's own pricing widget links to.</li>
    <li>Max drawdown is 6%, 8% or 10% depending on product — there is no single TFT drawdown rule.</li>
    <li>Funded-stage allocation caps are far lower than the headline: $300K on Classic 1-Step, $400K on Classic 2-Step, $600K on Royal, Royal Pro and Dragon.</li>
    <li>News trading is blocked on Royal and Dragon; Dragon also blocks weekend holding.</li>
    <li>Founded 2021. Payout disruptions and an ownership restructuring in 2023 remain a documented risk factor.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How the rules actually work</strong></h2>

<p><strong>There is no single TFT drawdown rule.</strong> Max drawdown is 6% on Classic 1-Step, 8% on Knight, Knight Pro and Classic 2-Step, and 10% on Royal, Royal Pro and Dragon. On a $100,000 Classic 2-Step that means an $8,000 static floor anchored at your starting balance: your equity may not print below $92,000 at any point, and that floor never moves regardless of how far into profit you go. On a $100,000 Classic 1-Step the same floor sits at $94,000 — $2,000 tighter for a fee that is $20 higher.</p>

<p><strong>Knight Pro is the outlier.</strong> Its 8% maximum drawdown is labelled "Relative" rather than "Static" in the dataset, which does not map onto the static/trailing distinction the rest of the industry uses, and the help-centre article that would define it is 404. Treat Knight Pro's drawdown as unverified until TFT restores that page.</p>

<p><strong>Daily drawdown</strong> is 3% on Knight, Knight Pro and Classic 1-Step, 4% on Classic 2-Step, and 5% on Royal, Royal Pro and Dragon. The basis differs too: Knight, Classic 2-Step and Dragon measure the daily cap on <em>balance</em>, while Classic 1-Step, Royal and Royal Pro measure it on <em>balance and equity</em> — an equity-based daily cap can breach on an open floating loss, a balance-based one cannot.</p>

<p><strong>Minimum trading days</strong> range from 1 (Knight Pro, Dragon) to 5 per phase (Knight, Royal, Royal Pro). Classic 1-Step and Classic 2-Step both require 3. Maximum trading days is "Unlimited" on every product and every phase — there is no expiry clock.</p>

<p><strong>No percentage consistency rule is published on any product.</strong> The nearest thing is a rule TFT calls "The Daily March", a minimum-profitable-days requirement rather than a percent-of-total-profit cap. Knight sets it at 5 days with 1.00% per day in Phase 1 and 2 days with 1.00% per day at the funded stage; Knight Pro at 3 days with 1.00% per day; Royal Pro at 5 days, then 3 days, then 2 days with 1% per withdrawal period. Both Classic products have the field blank.</p>

<p><strong>News trading is blocked on Royal and Dragon</strong> at every phase including the funded stage, and allowed on the other five. <strong>Weekend holding is blocked on Dragon</strong> only. Expert Advisors and trade copiers are permitted on all seven products. Overnight holding is not a field in TFT's dataset at all, and with the help centre down there is no reachable page that states the rule.</p>

<p><strong>Leverage</strong> is 1:30 on Knight, Knight Pro, Classic 1-Step and Royal Pro; 1:50 on Royal and Classic 2-Step; and 1:100 on Dragon. Dragon is the loosest on leverage and the tightest on everything else — three phases, no news, no weekend holding.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>True cost to break even</strong></h2>

<p>Break-even profit is the fee divided by your profit split — the trading gain you must produce before your share of it repays what you spent. The R-multiple sets that figure against the dollar value of your maximum drawdown. R below 1 means the firm lets you lose more getting there than you need to make. Every figure below is <code>computeTrueCost()</code> output from TFT's published prices — see <a href="/true-cost-of-prop-firm-challenges">the true cost of prop firm challenges</a> for the method.</p>

<p><strong>Five of TFT's seven products cannot be costed at all.</strong> Knight, Knight Pro, Royal, Royal Pro and Dragon publish "Up to 99%" as their payout percentage. That is a ceiling, not a rate, and a ceiling is not a divisor — there is no number to divide the fee by, so break-even profit and R-multiple are undefined for those five. The two Classic products are the only ones that state a base split (80%), and they are the only two tables on this page.</p>
