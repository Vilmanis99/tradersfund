<p><strong>There is no industry-wide prop firm pass rate. No regulator or official dataset publishes one, and the percentages that circulate without a source can't be checked.</strong> What does exist is narrower and more useful: four firms that publish their own outcome figures with a stated denominator, and the rules and prices that firms publish for every product they sell. This page uses only those two kinds of evidence. The firm figures keep each firm's own denominator and period. The rest we counted ourselves across 89 challenge products at 18 firms, all captured from the firms' own pages between 14 Oct 2026 and 20 Oct 2026.</p>

<div class="key-takeaways">
  <div class="title">Fast take</div>
  <ul class="wp-block-list">
    <li>The median list fee for a $100K evaluation is $452 across 52 one-off-priced tiers at 12 firms. CFD evaluations sit at $477, futures at $267.</li>
    <li>The four firms that publish a pass rate report 16.8% to 36.22% for a single evaluation (Topstep's 2025 Trading Combines at the low end, Take Profit Trader's 2025 Trading Tests at the high end). Counted per person instead, Topstep reports 51.8% and Tradeify 40.3%. They count different things, so they don't add up to an industry average.</li>
    <li>A funded account doesn't mean a payout. 33.3% of Topstep's 2025 funded-level participants received one; Tradeify's figure is 28.5% and My Funded Futures' is 39.2%.</li>
    <li>25 of 89 products refund the evaluation fee on every account size. None of the 22 futures products does.</li>
    <li>41 of 89 products publish a consistency percentage, including 21 of 22 futures products. Only 8 products allow expert advisors (EAs) without conditions.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How we counted</strong></h2>

<p>The dataset is the one behind our reviews: one record per product, with prices per account size, loss limits, drawdown type, profit split, payout timing, consistency rule, refund flag and trading-rule flags, each captured from the firm's own product, FAQ or help-centre page. For this page we kept every product captured in the last 30 days, which leaves 89 products at 18 firms. Five products fell outside that window, all last captured on 14 Sep 2026, and were left out: Apex Trader Funding's 4 products and Alpha Capital's Alpha Three, which the firm says is not currently offered for new purchases. The full product list, with a link to each source page, is in the table at the end.</p>

<p>Three rules govern every count below:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
  <li><strong>We count products, not firms.</strong> A firm that sells 9 variants weighs more than one that sells 2, so where firm-level numbers tell a different story we give both.</li>
  <li><strong>A blank isn't a zero.</strong> When a firm doesn't publish a value, the field stays empty in our data and appears here as "not published". It is never counted as "none". Our <a href="/blog/prop-firms-with-no-consistency-rule">no-consistency-rule guide</a> explains why that difference matters.</li>
  <li><strong>List prices only.</strong> Fees are the published price before coupons. Activation fees, fees on funding and monthly renewals are shown separately, never averaged in.</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Worked example: the profit you need just to get your fee back</strong></h3>

<p>You keep only your split of each payout, so the fee has to be divided by that split. At the overall evaluation median and an 80% split, $452 ÷ 0.80 = $565 of gross profit before you're even. That's 0.57% of a $100K balance. A futures trader at the median $267 with a 90% split needs $267 ÷ 0.90 = $296.67. On Topstep's Standard Path, one month plus activation is $99 + $149 = $248, so $248 ÷ 0.90 = $275.56. Each extra month adds $99 before any payout. Our <a href="/true-cost-of-prop-firm-challenges">true cost guide</a> carries this through resets and repeat attempts.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How to read a pass rate</strong></h3>

<p>Tradeify shows why the denominator is the whole story. Over the same 12 months, 17.2% of its evaluation accounts passed, yet 40.3% of the people buying them passed at least once, and the median participant started 3 evaluations. The per-account figure tells you the odds on one purchase; the per-person figure tells you the odds over a run of retries you pay for. Topstep's 16.8% and 51.8% split the same way.</p>

<p>The gotcha sits one step further on. Passing gets you a funded account, not a payout. Topstep's own figures say 33.3% of people at its Funded Level received a payout in 2025, and Tradeify's figure is 28.5%. Don't multiply the pass rate by the payout rate to get one "success" number. The groups overlap differently (someone funded in 2024 can be paid in 2025), and neither firm publishes how many attempts or how much money each person spent. Take Profit Trader also still shows an older figure in its FAQ: 20.37% of "currently registered users" passed a test between 1 Jan and 31 Aug 2023. That's a different period and a different denominator from its 2025 figure, so we don't compare the two. Our <a href="/blog/is-prop-firm-trading-profitable">profitability guide</a> turns these outcomes into a net-cash test.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Profit splits: 80% is the default, 90% is mostly futures</strong></h2>

<p>53 of 89 products start at an 80% base split. 17 start at 90%, and 15 of those are futures products. Lower bases mostly appear on instant or fast-payout plans: 5 products at 70%, 4 at 50% (including <a href="/go/tradeday?source=quick-pay-intraday&amp;from=post-body-prop-firm-statistics">TradeDay Quick Pay</a> and <a href="/go/city-traders-imperium?source=instant-funding&amp;from=post-body-prop-firm-statistics">City Traders Imperium Instant Funding</a>) and 2 at 75% (The5ers' Pro Growth and Hyper Growth). One product, <a href="/go/fundingpips?source=zero&amp;from=post-body-prop-firm-statistics">FundingPips Zero</a>, starts at 95%. 7 products have no single base split because the firm ties it to a payout option you choose later.</p>

<p>The two markets have opposite defaults. Most CFD products (39 of 56) use a static maximum loss: the floor is set from the starting balance and never moves. No futures product we track does. 19 of 22 trail the account's end-of-day high, and the other 3 trail intraday. On a trailing floor the limit rises with your high-water mark, so profit you give back eats into the room you have left. Our <a href="/blog/balance-based-drawdown-vs-equity-based-drawdown">drawdown guide</a> works through the mechanics.</p>

<p>Consistency rules split the same way. 21 of 22 futures products publish one; the exception is <a href="/go/my-funded-futures?source=builder&amp;from=post-body-prop-firm-statistics">My Funded Futures Builder</a>. Every product at Lucid Trading, Take Profit Trader, Topstep, TradeDay and Tradeify carries one. On the CFD side, only 17 of 56 publish a percentage, and at BrightFunded, City Traders Imperium, FundedNext, OFP Funding and The5ers no product in our capture publishes one at all. That still isn't proof that no rule exists, as explained above. Where a percentage is published, it ranges from 15% to 55% (<a href="/go/topstep?source=trading-combine-standard-path&amp;from=post-body-prop-firm-statistics">Topstep's Combine</a>). 40% is the most common, on 12 products. The rule can apply in the evaluation, at the funded stage or both, and a 40% rule means no single day may make up more than 40% of the profit it measures. Our <a href="/blog/what-is-prop-firm-consistency-rule">consistency rule guide</a> shows how each version is calculated.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>EAs and news trading: conditional is the norm</strong></h2>

<p>Our data marks a rule "allowed" only when the firm allows it without conditions. Conditions such as pre-approval, a platform exclusion or a blackout window put it under "conditional". By that test:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
  <li><strong>Expert advisors (automated trading bots):</strong> 8 products allow them outright (Lucid Trading's 7 and <a href="/go/city-traders-imperium?source=1-step-challenge&amp;from=post-body-prop-firm-statistics">City Traders Imperium 1-Step</a>), 63 allow them with conditions, 16 ban them and 2 don't say. FXIFY's phase challenges, for example, need pre-approval and never allow EAs on DxTrade.</li>
  <li><strong>News trading:</strong> 27 products allow it outright, 59 restrict it (typically a no-trade window around high-impact releases; FTMO's restriction, for example, applies only on funded Standard accounts, per its homepage FAQ), 1 bans it (<a href="/go/fundingpips?source=zero&amp;from=post-body-prop-firm-statistics">FundingPips Zero</a>) and 2 don't say.</li>
</ul>
<p>"Conditional" covers rules that range from a one-off approval email to a ban on specific strategies, so it isn't a green light. If you run an EA or trade releases, read the rule for the exact product and platform before you buy.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Fee refunds: a CFD perk, never a futures one</strong></h2>

<p>25 of 89 products refund the evaluation fee on every account size, usually with the first or a later reward once you're funded. They sit at 6 firms: BrightFunded, FTMO, FundedNext, FXIFY, Maven and OFP Funding. 51 products refund nothing, and for 13 the firm either doesn't say or the answer differs by size. Not one of the 22 futures products refunds its fee, and Tradeify's agreement, for example, calls every fee "strictly non-refundable".</p>

<p>The refund flag can change within a single firm at the same price. <a href="/go/fxify?source=two-phase-standard&amp;from=post-body-prop-firm-statistics">FXIFY Two Phase Standard</a> and <a href="/go/fxify?source=two-phase-classic&amp;from=post-body-prop-firm-statistics">FXIFY Two Phase Classic</a> both list $549 for $100K; Standard refunds the fee with the first payout and Classic refunds nothing. A refund also only arrives if you pass and get paid. Nothing comes back from a failed attempt. Our <a href="/blog/fxify-review">FXIFY review</a> covers the product-by-product refund rules.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Payout totals the firms publish</strong></h2>

<p><a href="/blog/ftmo-review">FTMO</a>'s homepage states "$650M+ Paid in rewards" and "4.5M+ Customers worldwide" (https://ftmo.com/en/, page last modified 20 Oct 2026, read 21 Oct 2026). Both are cumulative, self-reported marketing figures. They have no period breakdown, no count of how many customers were paid and no independent audit. Dividing one by the other wouldn't give an average payout, because "customers" includes everyone who ever bought a challenge or used the platform. We haven't built an industry total: totals compiled by third-party aggregators don't meet this page's sourcing rule.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Closures and rule changes we've logged</strong></h2>

<p>Our <a href="/prop-firms/closed">closed prop firms</a> list currently holds 4 firms recorded as closed and 1 recorded as offline, sources checked 20 Oct 2026. A firm goes on the list only when the firm itself, a regulator or a court says it closed. Press-only claims are marked with the outlet that reported them. That's a record of documented cases, not a count of every firm that has shut down. The circulating estimates of how many firms closed in a given year publish no counting method, so we don't repeat them.</p>

<p>Rules move faster than closures. Since 27 Jul 2026, our <a href="/prop-firm-challenge-changes">challenge changes log</a> has recorded 33 entries across 14 of the firms on this page:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
  <li><strong>10 verified rule changes,</strong> for example Topstep's Combine consistency target rising from 50% to 55% (observed 6 Oct 2026) and Take Profit Trader cutting new Test minimum days to three.</li>
  <li><strong>13 source conflicts,</strong> where a firm's own pages disagree with each other. 12 were still open at the last check.</li>
  <li><strong>8 price watches</strong> and <strong>2 lineup changes</strong> (products added or withdrawn).</li>
</ul>
<p>Thirteen cases of a firm's pages contradicting each other is the clearest argument for reading the exact product page on the day you buy, not a summary written last quarter, including this one.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What we left out, and why</strong></h2>

<p>Some numbers often repeated about the industry are missing here on purpose. We left them out because we couldn't trace them to a firm's own disclosure, a regulator or an official dataset on 21 Oct 2026:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
  <li><strong>An "industry" pass rate.</strong> No firm, regulator or official dataset publishes one, and the four firm figures above don't average into one.</li>
  <li><strong>An industry-wide share of funded traders who get paid.</strong> Only firm-level figures exist (33.3%, 28.5% and 39.2% above).</li>
  <li><strong>Industry payout totals and on-chain payout estimates.</strong> These are compiled by aggregators and trade media from firm-supplied or wallet data, not published by the firms or a regulator.</li>
  <li><strong>Counts of firms that closed in a year, and of active firms worldwide.</strong> The directory counts we found each define "active" their own way and don't agree, and the closure estimates publish no method.</li>
  <li><strong>Platform market share</strong> (for example, MetaTrader 5's share of accounts). We couldn't find the vendor's own publication of it.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>FAQ</strong></h2>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What percentage of traders pass a prop firm challenge?</strong></h3>
<p>No industry-wide figure exists. Four firms publish their own: 16.8% of Topstep Trading Combines started in 2025 were completed, 17.2% of Tradeify evaluation accounts passed between Aug 2025 and Jul 2026, 36.22% of Take Profit Trader Trading Tests passed in 2025, and 19.97% of My Funded Futures evaluation participants advanced (period not stated). Counted per person, not per account, Topstep's rate rises to 51.8% and Tradeify's to 40.3%.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How much does a $100K prop firm challenge cost in 2026?</strong></h3>
<p>The median list fee across 52 one-off $100K evaluation tiers at 12 firms is $452. CFD evaluations have a median of $477 and futures $267. Monthly-subscription futures plans, euro-priced plans and pay-later plans are listed separately, and activation fees are extra.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How many funded traders actually get a payout?</strong></h3>
<p>Among firms that publish it: 33.3% of Topstep's funded-level participants in 2025, 28.5% of Tradeify's between Aug 2025 and Jul 2026, and 39.2% of My Funded Futures' (period not stated). Each counts individuals at its own funded stage, so the three can't be combined into one rate.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Do prop firms refund the challenge fee?</strong></h3>
<p>Mostly not. 25 of the 89 products we track refund the fee on every account size, all at 6 firms that mainly sell CFD challenges, and only after you're funded and paid. None of the 22 futures products refunds its fee.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Is static or trailing drawdown more common?</strong></h3>
<p>It depends on the market. 39 of 56 CFD products use a static maximum loss, while all 22 futures products use a trailing one, 19 of them measured at the end of the day. Across all 89 products, 43 are static, 24 trail intraday and 21 trail at end of day.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How often do prop firms pay out?</strong></h3>
<p>On-demand is the most common cycle (39 of 89 products), driven by futures firms, followed by bi-weekly (31), monthly (6) and weekly (2). The earliest first request ranges from the same day to 30 days, and 28 products don't publish a fixed first-request date.</p>

<p>Before you quote any number from this page, check its row: the denominator, the period and the date we read it. Then open the firm's own page and confirm it still says the same thing.</p>
