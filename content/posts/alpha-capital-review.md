<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How the rules actually work</strong></h2>
<p><strong>Pro’s static limits depend on the target.</strong> Pro 6% has 6% targets in both phases, a 3% daily limit and a 6% static maximum loss. Pro 8% has 8% then 5% targets, a 4% daily limit and an 8% static maximum. Pro 10% has 10% then 5% targets, a 5% daily limit and a 10% static maximum. On a $100K Pro 6% account, the initial total-loss floor is $94,000; on Pro 10% it is $90,000. Changing the payout schedule does not transfer one target’s drawdown rules to another.</p>
<p><strong>One’s high-water rule follows balance and eventually locks.</strong> The One 10% help page gives a $100K example: the initial floor is $94,000; at a $102,000 maximum balance it becomes $96,000. At $106,000, the trailing floor locks at the original $100,000 balance and stops moving. It does not continue trailing equity indefinitely. Withdrawing all profit after this lock removes the operating buffer and can close the account even though a reward was paid.</p>
<p><strong>Direct has a separate 5% trailing path.</strong> A $100K Direct account starts with a $95,000 floor, which moves to $97,000 at a $102,000 maximum balance. Once balance reaches $105,000, the floor locks at $100,000. Its 3% daily limit is separate from the 5% total-loss allowance. Direct has no evaluation profit target, but that does not remove its funded-stage loss or payout gates.</p>
<p><strong>Daily loss is not always a fixed percentage of initial balance.</strong> One 10%, Pro 6%, Three and Direct use the higher of end-of-day balance or equity for their next daily calculation. Pro 8%, Pro 10% and Swing use balance-based daily limits. On a $100K Pro 6% account starting the day at its initial balance, 3% gives $3,000 of daily room; floating losses consume that room. The help centre applies a breach to current equity, so an open loss can close the account without a realised trade.</p>
<p><strong>The 40% Best Day Rule belongs to on-demand rewards.</strong> On-demand Pro, One and Swing require a minimum 2% gross profit and a best day no larger than 40% of the relevant profit total. A hypothetical $1,000 best day therefore needs at least $2,500 total profit, subject to the account-size minimum. That ratio describes reward eligibility on a Qualified account; it is not evidence that a $6,000 Pro 6% Phase 1 target imposes a $2,400 best-day cap. Bi-weekly consistency remains unverified in the data, rather than being copied from on-demand or labelled absent.</p>
<p><strong>Direct’s buffer and per-asset risk can bind first.</strong> The 15% best-day limit is a payout condition. Independently, its Max Risk Rule closes the account when open drawdown on one asset reaches 1%. Losing positions on that asset combine, profitable positions do not cancel those losses, and reopening the same asset in the same direction within 10 minutes combines losses toward the threshold. A $100K account therefore reaches that per-asset threshold at $1,000, before exhausting its broader 3% daily allowance.</p>
<p><strong>News permissions require the product help rule.</strong> One, Pro, Three and Direct restrict executing or closing trades during the 5 minutes before and after relevant high-impact news. Swing instead requires trades initiated in its 2-minute window on either side of an event to last longer than 2 minutes. The pricing article’s shorter evaluation-stage description does not replace these product-specific restrictions; confirm the stage and affected instrument before placing a news trade.</p>
