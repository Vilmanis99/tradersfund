<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How the rules actually work</strong></h2>

<p><strong>1-Step trailing drawdown.</strong> On a $100K account, the initial 6% maximum-loss floor is $94K. If equity reaches $104K, even as floating profit, the real-time high-water mark moves the floor to $98K; if that position reverses below $98K, the account breaches. The floor locks at $100K once equity reaches $106K. This is materially tighter than a static 6% loss allowance.</p>

<p><strong>2-Step static drawdown.</strong> On a $100K 2-Step Bright account, the 8% maximum-loss floor stays at $92K even after a profitable day; its daily loss rule is 4%. On the $100K Classic, the fixed floor is $90K under the 10% maximum-loss rule and the daily cap is 5%. Classic provides $2,000 more total loss room but requires a 10% first target instead of 8%.</p>

<p><strong>Minimum-day and consistency rules.</strong> Every evaluation phase requires 5 trading days, and one trade must remain open for at least 60 seconds for the day to count. Bright Funded says it has no consistency rule, so no single-day profit percentage controls passing or payout eligibility.</p>

<p><strong>News trading.</strong> Both evaluation phases allow news trading without a blackout. The funded account prohibits opening or closing—including stop loss, take profit, and pending-order execution—from 5 minutes before until 5 minutes after listed high-impact events. Profits from that 10-minute window are deducted, while losses remain; positions opened at least 48 hours earlier receive a take-profit exception.</p>

<p><strong>Holding, EAs, and copying.</strong> Overnight and weekend holding are allowed, although swaps count unless a swap-free add-on is purchased. Expert Advisors are permitted, but DXtrade does not support API or automated execution. Copy trading is limited to Bright Funded, other evaluation, or retail accounts owned by the same person; copying another trader is prohibited.</p>

<p><strong>India practical check.</strong> Bright Funded’s 2026-07-27 help page listed Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Pakistan, Syria, and Vietnam as restricted, not India. That supports purchase availability, not Indian regulatory approval. KYC still requires a valid passport, national ID, or driving licence after the evaluation, and an Indian trader should confirm name matching and payout-route availability before buying.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Payout speed in practice</strong></h2>

<p><strong>First request: day 30.</strong> The standard funded cycle starts when the first trade is placed, and the first Reward Split can be requested after 30 days. Subsequent standard requests run every 14 days. A 14-day first cycle, a 7-day weekly cycle, and a 90% split are checkout add-ons rather than base terms.</p>

<p><strong>Methods and minimum.</strong> Bright Funded publishes bank transfer in EUR and crypto in USDC on ERC-20, with no firm-imposed minimum beyond €0.01-equivalent profit. The firm says its finance team processes an approved request within 1 day, but banks, crypto processors, network gas, and currency conversion can add third-party costs.</p>

<p><strong>India cash-flow implication.</strong> An Indian trader paying a EUR fee and receiving an EUR bank transfer can face conversion at both ends; USDC avoids a bank FX conversion but adds ERC-20 network and off-ramp considerations. Bright Funded says third-party payout costs can range from $5 to $50 in some cases, so confirm the receiving route before a small first withdrawal.</p>

<p><strong>Independent-rating caveat.</strong> Trustpilot had suppressed Bright Funded’s aggregate rating when we captured it on 2026-07-27. That is different from a missing score: the public aggregate was unavailable, so this review does not use a star rating as evidence of payout reliability.</p>
