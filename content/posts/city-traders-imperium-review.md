<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>True cost to break even</strong></h2>

<p>Break-even profit is the fee divided by your profit split — the trading gain you must produce before your share of it repays what you spent. The R-multiple sets that figure against the dollar value of your maximum drawdown. R below 1 means the firm lets you lose more getting there than you need to make; R at or above 1 means you must out-earn your entire loss allowance just to get back to zero. <p><strong>Instant Funding pays a 50% split, and Direct Funding costs up to $3,999.</strong> Both push the R-multiple to the edge of viability. At the $2,500 Instant tier you need $158 of profit against $150 of total permitted loss — R = 1.05, meaning a trader who blows the account has lost more than the profit target they were chasing. Direct Funding sits at R = 1.19 across its $20K, $40K and $80K tiers. These are not marginal differences from the evaluation route: the 2-Step at $100K is R = 0.07, roughly seventeen times more forgiving than Direct Funding at the same drawdown percentage.</p>

<p>The structural reason is that both products charge a funded-account premium up front while halving or discounting the split. Skipping the evaluation is what you are buying, and at CTI that convenience costs between 10× and 17× on risk-adjusted terms. A trader who can pass a 1-Step at $100K pays $449 to reach an 80% split; reaching a comparable funded position through Direct Funding costs $3,999 at 70%.</p>
